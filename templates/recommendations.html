Practitioner & Patient Guidelines

Clinical Recommendations & Prevention

Evidence-based oral oncology protocols, symptom recognition matrices, early screening intervals, and specialist referral triggers.

Critical Trigger

Non-Healing Ulcers (> 2 Weeks)

Any solitary ulceration of the tongue, floor of mouth, or buccal mucosa persisting beyond 14 days without spontaneous resolution or traumatic explanation.

Action: Urgent referral for scalpel incisional biopsy.
High Risk PMOD

Erythroplakia & Leukoplakia

Persistent velvety red patches (erythroplakia, carrying up to 90% dysplasia risk) or non-wipeable white mucosal plaques (leukoplakia).

Action: Biopsy recommended within 7 to 10 days.
Physical Sign

Tissue Induration & Fixation

Firmness or palpable induration upon bimanual palpation, accompanied by decreased tongue motility or unexplained unilateral lymphadenopathy.

Action: Head and neck oncology staging workup.
Tobacco, Betel Quid & Alcohol Synergism

Smoking and smokeless tobacco (chewing tobacco, gutkha, paan with areca nut) expose mucosal keratinocytes to tobacco-specific nitrosamines (NNK, NNN). When combined with chronic alcohol consumption, ethanol acts as an organic solvent that dramatically increases mucosal permeability to chemical carcinogens, multiplying relative risk up to 35-fold.

7-Step Systematic Visual & Palpatory Protocol
1. Labial Mucosa & Vermilion Border: Evert upper and lower lips; inspect color, symmetry, and dryness.
2. Buccal Mucosa & Vestibule: Retract cheeks bilaterally; inspect Stensen's duct and retro-molar pads.
3. Lateral Borders of Tongue: Grasp tip with sterile gauze; gently retract left and right to inspect posterior thirds.
4. Floor of Mouth & Lingual Frenum: Instruct patient to touch palate with tongue tip; bidigitally palpate sublingual tissue.
Specialist Referral Pathway

Patients flagged by OralVision AI with high confidence for Oral Malignancy or Persistent Dysplasia should be immediately routed through a 2-Week Wait (2WW) referral to an Oral & Maxillofacial Surgery (OMFS) unit, ENT Head & Neck department, or designated Oncology Cancer Center.

Include specimen photographs, Grad-CAM attention visualizations, and medical history in clinical handover documents.
